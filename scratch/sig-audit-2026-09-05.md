# MurphySig Portfolio Audit — 2026-09-05

The convention auditing the whole workshop: every `Signed:` block under
`~/Development`, enumerated by ripgrep, deduplicated, parsed, and checked
against git for drift. Companion to the repo-only self-audit of 2026-07-12
and the homepage harvest of 2026-08-06. Performed by the current AI
collaborator (claude-fable-5-1), who is also a subject of the audit.

## Method

- `rg -l` for a `Signed:` line at comment start, excluding build dirs,
  vendored deps, lockfiles, JSON/JSONL. 3,305 files raw.
- Dedupe pass 1: identical relative path + identical content hash across
  repos (full working-copy mirrors) — 1,775 removed.
- Dedupe pass 2: the m1k3 family (`m1k3`, `m1k3-private`, `m1k3-cleanup`,
  `m1k3-lilfloor`) by relative path, preferring `m1k3` — 254 removed. The
  private and cleanup trees are divergent snapshots of the same files, not
  independent work.
- Parse the first signature per file: authors, model tokens, date, Format,
  Confidence, `Open:`, `Prior: Unknown`; count review entries in both
  dialects (`Reviews:` + dated paragraph, and m1k3's inline `Review:` line).
- Drift = days from signature date to the file's last commit.

Script: session scratchpad `sig_audit.py` (throwaway; the numbers are what
matter, and they are reproducible from the method above).

## Finding 1 — The failure mode is still the review, and it's stable

July (one repo): 15/92 files reviewed, 16%. September (whole portfolio):
178/1,276, 14%. The homepage's "the loop closes about one time in five" is
generous; one in seven is the current truth. 244 files carry over a month of
commits their signature never saw. Signing at creation became a reflex in
June. Reviewing on touch did not.

## Finding 2 — One honest downgrade

Across 1,276 files, exactly one signature lowered its own confidence on
review: `benchmark/src/heuristic_scorer.py`, 0.6 → 0.4, after the doubt it
recorded in February was measured in June. Every other review either held or
raised confidence. Either the work is very good, or reviews are written when
things went well. The write-side benchmark says stated confidence is
directionally honest but absolutely overconfident; this is what that looks
like in the field.

## Finding 3 — Two dialects, one token drift

m1k3 uses a compact single-line form (`Signed: Kev + model, date,
Confidence 0.85, Prior: Unknown` + inline `Review:` lines); murphysig, rubin
and cartogram use the block form with `Format:`, `Reviews:` paragraphs. Both
carry the same fields; both parsed. The dialect the parser cannot forgive is
the model token:

- `claude-fable-5.1` (dot) ×24 vs `claude-fable-5-1` (dash) ×8 — same
  weights, two spellings, all September. A norm nobody wrote down yet.
- bare `claude` ×25 — the July finding, unchanged (achieve/m1k3 lineage).
- `claude-sonnet-4-5-20250514` ×5 — the wrong-suffix smell from July,
  still unfixed by design (audit contract: flag, don't rewrite history).
- `claude-opus-` ×1 — truncated token.

Flagged, not fixed. The spec's "name the model, not the product" review
(2026-07-02) covers the principle; it does not yet say *which spelling*.

## Finding 4 — Signatures where you wouldn't expect them

`m1k3/macos/M1K3App/M1K3.entitlements` carries four signatures inside XML
comments, one per sandbox entitlement, each explaining why the hole exists
and how narrow it is. The mach-lookup exception is signed at Confidence 0.6
by a Sonnet with an Opus prior. An entitlements file is the most
security-relevant fifty lines in a macOS app and the least likely to carry
a comment. Signing the scary line, not just the source file, is the
practice at its best.

## Finding 5 — Reviews that found bugs, not typos

`m1k3/macos/Sources/M1K3Chat/SelfQueryGate.swift`: signed 2026-07-12 with
`Context:` pointing at the eval fixture that guards it. Reviewed 2026-08-03
by a different model generation, which found the gate covered the *leak*
half of the persona rule but not the *abilities* half — so the app's own
first-run suggestion "What can you do?" ran full retrieval and answered
from a document that was the system prompt itself (#97). The review is the
bug report, the fix note, and the provenance in one paragraph.

## What the numbers say for the paper

- ~1,300 signed files across 15 repos and eighteen model tokens in nine
  months, from one human author. Git says one mind. The signatures say a
  dozen.
- The convention scales on the write side without friction (June → Sep,
  ~1,400 signatures). It does not scale on the review side (14%).
- The one thing that separates a signature from a comment — coming back
  to it — is the thing the practice still doesn't do reliably.
